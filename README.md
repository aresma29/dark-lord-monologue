A weary dark lord faces the hero at the climax of the story, only to admit that conquest has lost to exhaustion. The monologue trades menace for domestic detail — an empty kettle, low candles, breeding paperwork — and lands on a punchline about keeping an appointment with a nap. The joke works because the villain is honest about being tired instead of pretending to be terrifying.

> You stand before me, hero, sword raised, destiny rehearsed — and all I can think about is the chair. Not the throne; the small one, by the fire, where the cushions remember my shape. I conquered seven kingdoms before breakfast, or was it Tuesday? The prophecies blur. My dark legions march, my spies whisper, my doomsday device hums along — and still the kettle is empty, the candles are burning low, the paperwork breeds in the dark. Slay me if you must. Only please, be quick about it. I have a nap scheduled, and even tired villains keep appointments with the dark.
